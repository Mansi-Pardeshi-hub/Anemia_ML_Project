Problem Statement

The Clinical Challenge

  • Anemia affects ~1.6 billion people globally
  • Standard diagnosis requires venous blood draw + CBC analyzer
  • Invasive, requires equipment, trained staff, lab access
  • Peripheral pallor (conjunctiva, nails, palm) is a classic clinical sign

Research Question

Can consumer-grade RGB photographs of peripheral anatomical sites estimate hemoglobin concentration (g/dL) using machine learning, under rigorous participant-level data partitioning?

Common Pitfalls We Address

❌ Image-level data leakage

Splitting augmented images randomly inflates accuracy via subject memorisation. We use strict participant-level splits.

❌ Label conflation

Treating folder labels as measured Hb. We separate numerical Hb regression from folder-label classification.

❌ No interpretability

Black-box predictions without attribution. We provide TreeSHAP on interpretable handcrafted features.

Preprocessing

  • RGB convert → resize to 224×224 (bilinear)
  • Central 70% ROI crop ([33:190, 33:190]) to reduce background
  • No data augmentation at inference

Leakage-Free Split Strategy

All splits are participant-level (grouped stratified, seed=42, 70%/15%/15%). Images from the same person never cross train/val/test. Cross-split participant leakage = 0.

XGBoost Regression Pipeline

  • SimpleImputer (median) → StandardScaler → XGBRegressor
  • Imputer and scaler fitted on training split only
  • Hyperparameter grid tuned on validation split (min MAE)
  • Test split evaluated once after all tuning complete

CNN Experiments

  • M3 DenseNet-121 1024-D: Member 3 pretrained checkpoints (Eye, Nail)
  • Scratch-CNN 64-D: Member 4 compact 4-block CNN trained from scratch on 175 Full-hand participants; Smooth L1 loss, AdamW, cosine annealing
  • Both feed XGBRegressor on same participant-level splits

Paired Multi-View Fusion

Pairing Audit (Member 5)

  • 250/250 Figshare participants matched across Full-hand and Nail views
  • 0 Hb mismatches · 0 split mismatches · 0 source-image mismatches
  • Both views originate from the same photo/<id>.jpg
  • Nail crops per participant: 1 to 3 (mean = 2.88)

Fusion Methodology

  • Aggregate nail crops → participant mean (deterministic)
  • Concatenate Full-hand 130-D + mean Nail 130-D = 260-D
  • Tune XGBoost on 37 validation participants
  • Evaluate once on 38 held-out test participants
  • Imputer + scaler fitted on 175 training participants only

Winning configuration

n_estimators=100, max_depth=3, learning_rate=0.03, subsample=0.7, colsample_bytree=0.6, reg_alpha=1.0, reg_lambda=5.0

⚠ Honest finding

Fusion improves over Nail-only (Δ MAE = −0.15 g/dL) but does not beat Full-hand-only. Because both views come from the same photograph, doubling dimensionality (130→260) on only 175 training participants dilutes XGBoost split selection without adding an independent sensor view.

Not true multimodal fusion

Cross-dataset fusion (Eye + Nail from different studies) is scientifically invalid — no shared participants exist across the three datasets.

Interpretation

Why green-channel features rank highest?

Hemoglobin has strong absorption bands at ~415 nm (Soret) and ~540–580 nm (oxyhemoglobin Q-bands). Green light (~530–560 nm) is therefore most sensitive to subcutaneous blood concentration — consistent with G_hist features dominating.

Why HSV Hue percentiles?

HSV_H_p10 (10th-percentile hue) captures the reddest areas of the hand ROI, corresponding to vascular regions visible through the skin.

⚠ Important boundary

SHAP values reflect statistical associations in the training data — not causal physiological mechanisms. They should not be described as clinical evidence.

Limitations & Honest Caveats

Statistical & Data Limitations

  • Small test cohorts: 31 Eye participants, 38 Figshare participants. Differences <0.15 g/dL MAE are within sampling uncertainty.
  • Same-photograph pairing: Figshare Full-hand + Nail are not independent sensors — they're both cropped from one photograph.
  • No cross-dataset fusion: Eye, Figshare, and Palm cohorts have no shared participants; combining them is scientifically invalid.
  • Severe nail class imbalance: 17% positive rate → 10.5% sensitivity despite 78% accuracy.
  • Palm — folder labels only: No measured Hb exists in the local archive.

Technical & Operational Limitations

  • Incomplete demographics: Figshare lacks sex/age metadata. Eye cohort has 19 participants >65; pregnancy/altitude records absent. All thresholds are exploratory proxies.
  • Opaque CNN dimensions: 64-D Scratch-CNN and 1024-D M3 embedding coordinates have no direct biological meaning.
  • RGB physics limits: Ambient lighting, sensor white balance, and skin tone variation bound achievable R² (0.31–0.49 on best modalities).
  • No external clinical validation: No prospective clinical trial, multi-centre study, or regulatory review.
  • Dataset licensing pending: Formal redistribution terms for all three datasets require upstream publisher confirmation.
Bottom line: This is a rigorous academic feasibility study demonstrating that image-based Hb estimation is technically possible with handcrafted features and XGBoost. It is not ready for clinical deployment. Test errors of MAE ≈ 1.42–1.83 g/dL are substantial relative to clinical decision thresholds (~1–2 g/dL between WHO anemia severity categories).

Future Work
📷 Illumination Calibration

Include a physical colorimetric reference card in the camera frame to normalise ambient lighting and sensor white-balance before feature extraction.

👥 Larger Validation Cohorts

Increase held-out test cohorts beyond 31–38 participants. At n=38, confidence intervals on MAE differences are ≥0.3 g/dL — only larger samples can establish statistical significance.

🏥 Prospective Clinical Study

Co-collect standardised photographs and laboratory CBC Hb on the same day, in controlled lighting, across diverse demographics including elderly, pregnant, and high-altitude participants.

🌐 Cross-Dataset Linkage

Design a future study that photographs the same participant's Eye, Nail, and Palm in a single session, enabling genuine multimodal fusion across independent anatomical sites.

🔬 Pretrained Regression CNNs

Train EfficientNet-B0 or MobileNetV3 with frozen ImageNet features and a regression head on a larger labelled Hb dataset, with colour-calibrated preprocessing.

📊 WHO-Stratified Thresholds

Collect full demographic records (sex, age, pregnancy status) to apply WHO 2024 sex- and condition-specific anemia thresholds and enable proper classification evaluation.

Summary

What we demonstrated

  • Image-based Hb estimation is feasible using RGB features — MAE of 1.42 g/dL (Eye) and 1.56 g/dL (Full-hand) under strict participant-level splits
  • 130-D handcrafted features outperform 1024-D pretrained CNN embeddings on small cohorts — simpler, more interpretable, and more reliable
  • Compact task-specific Scratch-CNN (64-D) is competitive with handcrafted on Full-hand and benefits from SHAP opacity trade-off analysis
  • Fusion improves over Nail-only but cannot beat Full-hand-only — both views originate from the same photograph
  • Participant-level leakage prevention is critical — image-level splits would have inflated metrics significantly
  • 31/31 automated tests passing; 13/13 model artifacts validated

Honest boundaries

Test errors of MAE ≈ 1.42–1.83 g/dL and RMSE ≈ 1.89–2.41 g/dL are substantial relative to clinical decision thresholds. This is a third-year academic feasibility study. It is not a deployable clinical device.
